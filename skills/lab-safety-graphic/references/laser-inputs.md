# Laser-hazard graphics

Use assembly ICDs to identify the laser sources and locate them inside racks, cabinets, or enclosures. Read both the equipment list and the associated rack figures; a beam destination is not necessarily the laser source location.

Capture, when explicitly provided:

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Laser/system identifier | Matches the map marker and source figure |
| Rack/cabinet and face | Identifies where the source is physically located |
| Rack units, shelf, or compartment | Supports the rack-detail inset |
| Wavelengths and source/output role | Separates fundamental, converted, and delivered beams |
| Operating state | Separates normal enclosed operation from alignment/service/open-beam work |
| Class, access restrictions, eyewear/OD, hazard boundary | Include only when established in the supplied safety source |
| Interlocks, emergency controls, response contacts | Copy location and action wording from current site material |
| Revision and figure/table/section | Keeps the callout auditable |

Do not infer laser class, required optical density, safe viewing distance, or an exposure boundary from wavelength, rack position, or a photograph alone. Do not label the whole lab as an exposure zone solely because it contains lasers. Do not assume a visible enclosure makes alignment or service exposure equivalent to normal operation.

When the available evidence covers only equipment identity and placement, make a clearly labeled location draft and ask for the missing safety facts needed to complete the hazard graphic. Continue extracting the rack figures and arranging the confirmed locations while waiting. Missing photos can be represented by useful ICD figures; do not invent photographs.

Use project safety documentation or an explicit user-provided instruction for response actions and PPE wording. If external technical verification is necessary, use primary sources, distinguish them from the site's approved rules, and do not present a generic web rule as the lab's procedure.

Keep the final sheet concise: central location graphic, a few source-backed rack details, short factual labels, and the supplied response/contact information. A one-page map does not replace the underlying laser safety procedure.
